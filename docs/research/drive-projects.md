## 2. ASCEND

**Live:** https://ascend-app-one.vercel.app/ · **Tagline used in the final docs:** *"The Strategic Operating System for Recruiting College Students"*

### 2.1 Origin story

`[PUBLIC SAFE]` Ascend began in **early February 2026** as the team venture for **BUS 160** at Washington & Lee, with Drake, **Carter Stack**, and **Emma Guillory**. The team contract is dated Feb 3, 2026 (`19SJm6TCFMsgBYwzaii-XTKVKeaMddhyPQnoGGNffOI4`); the first Pecha Kucha idea-generation notes are dated Feb 3–17.

`[PUBLIC SAFE]` The problem was first-hand, not researched. From the final reflection:

> "Ascend came from a problem I knew well because I had lived it. College gives you more freedom and free time, but it does not hand you a better way to organize your day-to-day. Once classes, recruiting, networking, clubs, workouts, meetings, and actual responsibilities start piling up, the issue is not just discipline anymore; it's that everything is completely scattered. Academic deadlines are in Canvas. Everyday commitments live in Google Calendar. Interviews are in Outlook. Coffee chats are in Outlook and Gmail. Then there is whatever Apple note or to-do list you swore you would keep up with but stopped checking days later."
> — *Draft #2 Ascend Final Reflection, 2026-03-31*

`[PRIVATE CAREER CONTEXT]` The Showcase reflection is blunter about where the idea came from — he writes about himself in the third person:

> "This idea did not come from a sleep-deprived nap dream or divine intervention, just a solution to a problem recruiting Drake felt firsthand, and it was one of the symptoms of his downfall."
> — *Showcase & Summit Reflection, 2026-04-07*

(That "downfall" line is candid self-criticism about his own recruiting cycle. Use the sentiment, not the sentence.)

`[PUBLIC SAFE]` His framing of why now: *"Recruiting timelines continue to accelerate to the point that now recruiting for finance begins the minute you set foot on campus, your freshman year."*

### 2.2 What was actually built and shipped vs. designed-only

`[PUBLIC SAFE]` **Shipped and live** (Progressive Web App — React, Vite, Tailwind CSS, deployed on Vercel; PWA chosen because "all integrations work from a browser, eliminates app store delay, and reflects that most recruiting work happens on laptops"):
- Unified dashboard with tabbed interface — **Academic / Personal / Recruiting**
- **Weekly Command Center** — urgency-ordered deadlines, Sunday preview
- **Archetype onboarding quiz** with personality-matched coaching (**Eagle / Fox / Bear**) and an accountability "pet" with selectable humor styles (e.g. sarcastic, hype man)
- Recruiting CRM with **AI-assisted thank-you email drafting**
- Syllabus-PDF upload with AI parsing (built as the workaround after the Canvas API was blocked)
- Personal to-do tab; intro animation showing scattered apps converging into one

`[PUBLIC SAFE]` **Designed / placeholder only:**
- Google Calendar and Outlook integrations — "placeholders pending API activation, but illustrating what these sections would look like." Rated "Easy" and "Easy-Medium" feasibility (REST + OAuth 2.0 / Microsoft Graph); ready to activate, not activated.
- Canvas LMS integration — **blocked** (see below).
- Apple Calendar — rated "Hard, no OAuth/REST path"; explicitly skipped for MVP, users directed to sync via Google.
- Stripe payment tiers, gamified learning, 4 additional archetypes (Wolf, Owl, Falcon, Lion), Handshake/LinkedIn integration, React Native app, multi-school expansion — all future phases.

`[PUBLIC SAFE]` **The Canvas / W&L ITS outcome** — this is one of the most interesting and most credible parts of the story:
- Request submitted **March 24, 2026** to W&L ITS for a Canvas LMS developer key (W&L-only integration), with a company overview and privacy policy attached.
- Response **April 3, 2026** from **Scott Eversole**, ITS, after a multi-team review including the Canvas Administrator. **Declined.**
- ITS said the team was *"impressed by the thought, effort, and level of detail"* but raised four blockers: no legal entity / no signed Data Use Agreement; FERPA (the "legitimate educational interest" exception does not apply to student-built third-party apps); infrastructure (Vercel KV is not a FERPA-covered entity); and sustainability (no succession plan for when the founders graduate).
- Path to reconsideration given: form a legal entity, secure a university sponsor, migrate OAuth token storage to a FERPA-compliant environment (e.g. Azure inside W&L's tenant), complete a formal security review, align with W&L's Technology ToS.
- His own read on it: *"This outcome is meaningful: the concept is sufficiently credible to warrant formal institutional review, and the barriers are operational rather than technical."*
- Note: the ITS correspondence itself lives in `ascend_it_email.docx`; the summary above is quoted from the Write-Up's Appendix E. `[PUBLIC SAFE]` — but naming Scott Eversole publicly is a judgment call; safer to say "W&L ITS."

### 2.3 User research findings

`[PUBLIC SAFE]` **Five stakeholder interviews**, split into two types (per `Ascend Interviews`, March 19, 2026):

**Persona interviews (2)** — Williams Investment Society students:
- **Charlie Lewis '28** (committed to investment banking): uses Excel + Google Calendar; needed something that tracks *coffee chats*, not just deadlines. Quoted in the deck: *"Even with Canvas synced to Google Calendar, assignment changes caused me to miss deadlines. I want AI that tracks across my email too."*
- **Dallas Bolen '28** (accounting, still exploring): finds CPD notifications inside Canvas "hard to manage properly and easy to overlook." Quoted: *"I use GoodNotes for exams and my Notes app for daily to-dos. It's tedious. I want something consistent across all my devices."*

**User tests (3)** — unguided interaction with the live prototype:
- **Wiley Rothrock** (premed sophomore, not recruiting): Apple Calendar + Outlook + Canvas; finds out about deadlines "right before." Called the accountability pet *"like Jarvis."* Independently named **automated thank-you emails after networking calls** as the feature he'd most pay for. *"If I was actively recruiting, I would probably pay for this."*
- **Zak Silnutzer** (premed sophomore): physical planner + Canvas; spends **20–30 minutes a week** just verifying deadlines — *"enough time to finish a small assignment"* — and lost points last semester on a missed planner entry. On the Sunday command center: *"That's perfect. That's awesome. I wish I had something like that."* Independently proposed **matching users with complementary archetypes as accountability partners.**
- **Lily Ha** (sophomore, actively recruiting for consulting — the single most valuable data point): Google Sheets + Calendar + Docs, describes herself as not organized. Said Ascend would *"force me to get things done."* **Pushed back** on the AI syllabus-parsing workaround — syllabi are mostly course descriptions, not structured schedules, and professors change them — preferring reliable manual upload with a Sunday summary. Also flagged that the recruiting tab has to beat Handshake and CPD-in-Canvas to justify paying.

`[PUBLIC SAFE]` **Cross-interview findings:**
- 100% used **three or more** disconnected platforms; 100% described their system as "scattered"; none used a single unified tool.
- **Weekly prioritization / the command center was the most universally validated feature** — every user expressed excitement or said they wished they had it now.
- Recruiting creates a *distinct second layer of urgency* that academic tools do not address.
- Willingness to pay is tied specifically to the recruiting functions.
- **Two features were independently invented by two different users** — AI thank-you emails and complementary-archetype social matching. He treats that convergence as a strong prioritization signal.
- **The one thing that did not validate:** archetype conviction during the quiz. All three hesitated; one said *"I am not any of those"* before the reveal — yet all confirmed the *result* felt accurate. His conclusion: the quiz wording, not the archetype system, is the problem.

`[PUBLIC SAFE]` Note on internal inconsistency: the final Write-Up body says *"Three interviews with W&L students conducted during prototype testing"*, but its own Appendix B lists **five** people and the interviews doc is explicit that there were 2 persona + 3 user tests = 5. **Use "five" — it is the better-supported number.**

### 2.4 Funding

> "CES shall provide an initial grant of **$2,866.00**, which should be paid within one month of this agreement… CES does not expect to derive any future revenue from this grant to the Business."

- It is a **grant, not equity** — non-dilutive.
- $2,866 is exactly the Year 1 infrastructure ask in his CES deck, so the number came from a costed line-item budget, not a round figure.
- Conditions: funds restricted to approved business uses; **financial reports required at the end of every semester for two years**; unspent funds returned if the business stops operating.
- The deck's honest framing was that $2,866 is infrastructure only, and *"a realistic Year 1 total"* including legal, design, and marketing is **~$5,066**.
- `[PUBLIC SAFE]` phrasing recommendation: **"$2,866 seed grant from W&L's Connolly Entrepreneurship Society"** — the exact figure is more credible than "~$3,000."

### 3.5 What he learned about behavior change

`[PUBLIC SAFE]` This is where the depth is. He treated retention as an engineering discipline and researched it properly (`Habit App Launch Research`, June 2026; `PWA and Native Transition Strategy`, July 2026):

- **The benchmark reality he was designing against:** ~77% of users abandon within three days; Day-7 retention medians of 8–15% (12–16% in health & fitness).
- **The "First 60 Seconds Rule"** — get the user to a core value transaction in under a minute. Each mandatory text field in setup costs 10–15% drop-off. His decision: **defer registration until Day 3**, use a guest token, get into the daily reflection in under 30 seconds.
- **Streak fragility and the "what-the-hell effect"** — a single missed day destroys the psychological asset and often the user. Fixes he adopted: **grace days** (3 built into V1), a **decoupled streak counter** (a one-tap check-in preserves the streak on a bad day — separating streak preservation from long-form journaling raises ≥7-day-streak maintenance by >40%), and **earned saves** rather than automatic forgiveness, because *"if streak freezes are granted automatically or without user investment, the perceived value of the streak is diminished."*
- **Notification design** — he moved to a **push primer** before the native iOS permission dialog (which is single-shot and unrecoverable if rejected), behavior-inferred trigger windows rather than user-selected times, a two-type system separating routine reminders from streak-save alerts, and **auto-throttling**: stop reminders after 4 days of inactivity with one respectful note, because *"excessive reminders to inactive users increase irritation and trigger app uninstalls."*
- **Why SMS became the exclusive channel** — 98% open rate vs ~20% for email, ~90% read within 3 minutes, works on every device with no install, no permission prompt, no Add-to-Home-Screen friction, ~$0.008/message via Twilio. TCPA consent checkbox and STOP handling designed in from the start.
- **The Day-8 iOS storage purge** — he identified that WebKit deletes all local PWA data (IndexedDB, service worker caches) after 7 days of inactivity, which would silently destroy returning users' data. He named this a "make-or-break retention constraint" and specified cloud rehydration on cold launch as the mitigation.
- **Ephemeral vs persistent journaling** — his most interesting idea: a **dual-track reflection toggle**, "Release" (text visually dissolves, nothing stored — removes self-censorship) vs "Record" (encrypted, archived, enables longitudinal pattern recognition). He cites both the psychological-safety literature and the "media externalization effect."
- **Against chat-as-interface** — *"Every major product initiative that tries to replace graphical interfaces entirely with conversational chat inputs introduces significant cognitive load."* His conclusion: ground the daily loop in structured direct manipulation; reserve generative/conversational UI for personalization and background tasks.
- **The buddy system thesis** — peer accountability as the drop-off countermeasure: paired nudges on completion, **mutual streaks that celebrate shared consistency rather than solitary performance**, and a trainer dashboard so Joe can see which pairs are thriving and which need a personal check-in.

`[PUBLIC SAFE]` Real-user feedback he logged and acted on (V1 Feedback sheet): a day-1 journal entry bleeding into the day-2 text box (fixed, high priority); logo clarity (reverted at Shawn's request); a streak that failed to advance to day 3 for one of Joe's clients; and a request to key the daily letter to the **calendar day** rather than the user's personal streak day, so the whole community does the same practice together.
